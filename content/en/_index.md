### Today's AI News

1.  Claude Code just dropped a brand-new **"Learning Mode"** 🧠! This awesome feature aims to tackle the dreaded **"AI brain atrophy"** problem, which can hit users who rely too much on AI. Instead of just handing out answers, it acts like a coding "fitness coach," guiding users to pause at key steps and get hands-on with tasks. The goal? To truly master programming skills. 💪

2.  Google's just open-sourced **Gemma 3 270M** 🤯, a super lightweight large model with only 270M parameters! But don't let the size fool you – it absolutely crushes **instruction following** and **text structuring** tasks, even outperforming some much larger models. Thanks to its low power consumption, this little powerhouse can run on devices like phones or Raspberry Pis, making it perfect for building nimble, fast, and budget-friendly AI apps. 🚀

3.  OpenAI's CEO, Sam Altman, just laid out a mind-blowing roadmap for the company 🤯. OpenAI is looking to invest trillions of dollars into building data centers and developing new AI hardware, even diving into **brain-computer interfaces**! The goal? To totally shake up search and social media. Altman reckons we're in a pivotal AI era, kind of like the dot-com bubble days, and the GPT-5 release marks "the best of times, the worst of times." Talk about ambition! ✨

5.  Check out **Hi3DEval**! 😎 This new, super scientific and comprehensive 3D generation quality assessment system was cooked up by the Shanghai AI Lab in partnership with several universities. It uses a three-tier evaluation protocol—object-level, component-level, and material themes—to dive deep into 3D models. It offers multi-dimensional analysis of overall shape, local structure, and material realism. Pretty neat, huh? 12. There's a fascinating debate brewing between Fei-Fei Li and Geoffrey Hinton on AI safety issues 🤔. Fei-Fei Li is all about **design, governance, and values**, while Hinton is more concerned about superintelligence going rogue, suggesting we design **AI that cares about humanity**. He highlights "goal misgeneralization" and "instrumental convergence" as two core concepts for potential AI risks. Food for thought! 🤖
