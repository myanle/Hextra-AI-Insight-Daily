### **Today's AI News**

1.  **Claude Code** just dropped a brand-new **"Learning Mode"** to tackle the dreaded "AI brain drain" that comes from relying too much on AI. Instead of just spitting out answers, this mode acts like your personal coding gym coach, pausing at key steps and guiding you to actually *do* the work yourself, so you truly master those programming skills. 🧠✨

2.  **Gemma 3 270M**, Google's open-source lightweight large model, boasts a mere 270M parameters but absolutely crushes it in **instruction following** and **text structuring** tasks, even outperforming some much larger models. Its low-power footprint means it can run on devices like phones or Raspberry Pis, making it perfect for building fast, cheap, and snappy AI applications. 🚀📱

3.  **OpenAI's CEO, Sam Altman**, just spilled the beans on a massive blueprint for the company: think trillions of dollars invested in building data centers and developing next-gen AI hardware, even diving into **brain-computer interfaces** to completely shake up search and social media. He reckons we're in a crucial AI era, kinda like the dot-com bubble, and GPT-5's release signals that "it's the best of times, it's the worst of times." 💰🤯

4.  **Xinzhiyuan** is celebrating its 10th anniversary and kicking off the ASI era! They're on the hunt for AI industry lead writers, senior video editors, senior editors/editors, and editorial interns. If you're an AI enthusiast, they'd love to have you onboard! It uses a super scientific and comprehensive approach with object-level, component-level, and material-theme evaluation protocols to analyze 3D models across multiple dimensions, checking out their overall form, local structure, and material realism. 11. **DeepMind's Chief Scientist, Denny Zhou**, recently broke down the **Large Language Model (LLM)** inference mechanism at Stanford's CS25 course. 12. **Li Feifei and Hinton** have some differing views on AI safety. Li Feifei is all about **design, governance, and values**, while Hinton is more worried about superintelligence running wild, suggesting we design AI that **cares about humanity**. "Goal misgeneralization" and "tool convergence" are two key concepts framing these potential AI risks. 🤔💡
