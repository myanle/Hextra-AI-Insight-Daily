### **Today's AI News**

1.  🚀 **Claude Code** has rolled out a brand-new **"Learning Mode"** to tackle the issue of **"AI skill decay"** caused by over-reliance on AI. Instead of just handing out answers, this mode acts like a coding "fitness coach," guiding users to pause at crucial steps and complete tasks themselves to truly master their programming chops.

2.  ✨ **Gemma 3 270M**, an open-source lightweight large model from Google, is making waves! With only 270M parameters, it totally crushes **instruction following** and **text structuring** tasks, even outperforming some much larger models. Its super low power consumption means it can run on devices like phones or Raspberry Pis, making it perfect for building nimble, speedy, and budget-friendly AI applications.

3.  💰 **OpenAI** CEO Sam Altman has laid out a mind-blowing grand vision for the company: think trillions of dollars invested in building data centers and developing next-gen AI hardware, even diving into **brain-computer interfaces** to totally shake up search and social media. He reckons we're smack dab in the middle of a pivotal AI era, kind of like the dot-com bubble, and the upcoming GPT-5 launch signals that "it's the best of times, it's the worst of times."

4.  🥳 **XinZhiYuan** is celebrating its tenth anniversary, marking the dawn of the ASI era! They're on the hunt for AI industry lead writers, senior video editors, senior editors/editors, and editorial interns. If you're passionate about AI, they'd love for you to join their crew! 5.  ✅ **Hi3DEval**, a super scientific and comprehensive 3D generation quality assessment system, has been launched by the Shanghai AI Laboratory in collaboration with several universities. It uses a three-tier evaluation protocol—object-level, component-level, and material theme—to analyze 3D models across multiple dimensions, checking their overall form, local structure, and material realism. Li Feifei is all about **design, governance, and values**, while Hinton is more worried about superintelligence going rogue, suggesting we cook up "human-caring AI." The two big concepts for potential AI risks? "Goal misgeneralization" and "instrumental convergence."
