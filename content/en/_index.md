### **Today's AI News Bytes**

1.  Claude Code just dropped a fresh "Learning Mode" 🤯! It's designed to tackle that scary "AI brain atrophy" from relying too much on AI. Instead of handing out answers, this mode acts like your personal coding gym buddy, prompting you to pause and get hands-on at crucial steps. The goal? To truly nail those programming skills.

2.  Gemma 3 270M, Google's open-source lightweight large language model, is quite the performer! 🚀 Weighing in at just 270M parameters, it excels at "instruction following" and "text structuring" tasks, even outperforming some heftier models. Its low-power footprint means it can run on devices like phones or Raspberry Pis, making it perfect for building quick, cost-effective, and nimble AI apps.

3.  OpenAI's CEO, Sam Altman, is painting a grand picture for the company's future 😲! His ambitious blueprint involves trillion-dollar investments in data centers and new AI hardware, even venturing into "brain-computer interfaces" to shake up search and social media. Altman believes we're smack dab in a critical AI era, reminiscent of the dot-com bubble, with the upcoming GPT-5 launch signaling "the best of times and the worst of times."

5.  Hi3DEval is a game-changer! ✨ Developed by the Shanghai AI Lab in collaboration with multiple universities, it's a more scientific and comprehensive quality assessment system for 3D generation. This system utilizes a three-tier evaluation protocol—object-level, component-level, and material theme—to conduct multi-dimensional analysis of 3D models' overall form, local structure, and material realism. [🔗 Hi3DEval Project Homepage](https://zyh482.github.io/Hi3DEval/) [🔗 3D Generation Leaderboard](https://huggingface.co/spaces/3DTopia/3DGen-Leaderboard)

Li Feifei and Hinton have some differing views on AI safety concerns, which is super interesting! 🤔 Li Feifei is all about "design, governance, and values," while Hinton's main worry is uncontrolled superintelligence, suggesting we design "AI that cares about humanity." The two core concepts for potential AI risks are "goal misgeneralization" and "tool convergence."
