### Today's AI News 🚀

1.  Claude Code just dropped a fresh **"Learning Mode"** to tackle that pesky **"AI brain drain"** from relying too much on AI. This mode isn't about spoon-feeding answers; instead, it's like having a coding personal trainer that nudges users to pause at key steps and get hands-on, really mastering those programming skills.

2.  Google just open-sourced **Gemma 3 270M**, a lightweight large language model packing only 270M parameters. This little powerhouse shines in **instruction following** and **text structuring** tasks, even outperforming some heftier models. Its low-power vibe means it can run on devices like phones or Raspberry Pis, making it perfect for building quick, cost-effective, and super lightweight AI apps.

3.  OpenAI CEO Sam Altman just laid out a massive vision for **OpenAI**, aiming to pour trillions into building data centers and developing new AI hardware. They're even looking to jump into the **brain-computer interface** game, planning to totally disrupt search and social media. Altman reckons we're in a critical AI phase, kinda like the dot-com bubble, with GPT-5's launch signaling "the best of times, the worst of times."

4.  Xinzhiyuan is kicking off its 10th-anniversary celebration, marking the dawn of the ASI era! They're on the hunt for AI industry reporters, senior video editors, senior editors/editors, and editorial interns. If you're passionate about AI, they'd love for you to join their crew! 5.  **Hi3DEval**, a new 3D generation quality evaluation system, was just rolled out by Shanghai AI Lab in collaboration with several universities. It's designed to be more scientific and comprehensive, using a three-tiered assessment protocol covering object-level, component-level, and material themes. This allows for multi-dimensional analysis of a 3D model's overall form, local structure, and material realism. Check out the [🔗 Hi3DEval Project Homepage](https://zyh482.github.io/Hi3DEval/) and the [🔗 3D Generation Leaderboard](https://huggingface.co/spaces/3DTopia/3DGen-Leaderboard)!

12. Li Feifei and Hinton have some differing views on AI safety. Li Feifei focuses on **design, governance, and values**, while Hinton is more worried about superintelligence running amok, suggesting we design **AI that actually cares about humanity**. "**Goal misgeneralization**" and "**instrumental convergence**" are two core concepts highlighting potential AI risks.
