### **Today's AI News**

1.  **Claude Code** launched its brand-new **"Learning Mode"** 💪 to tackle "AI brain atrophy" caused by over-reliance on AI. This mode doesn't just hand over answers; instead, it acts like a coding "personal trainer," nudging users to pause at key steps and get hands-on to truly master programming skills.

2.  Google open-sourced **Gemma 3 270M** 💡, a lightweight large model with only 270M parameters. This little powerhouse excels at **instruction following** and **text structuring**, even outperforming some much larger models. Its low-power design means it can run on devices like phones or Raspberry Pis, perfect for building quick, cost-effective, and light AI apps.

3.  **OpenAI** CEO Sam Altman has laid out a massive vision 🔭 for the company, outlining a plan to invest trillions of dollars into building data centers and developing new AI hardware. OpenAI even aims to dive into **brain-computer interface** tech, with the goal of completely shaking up search and social media. Altman believes we're in a critical AI era, much like the dot-com bubble, and the upcoming GPT-5 release marks "the best of times, and the worst of times."

4.  **Xinzhiyuan** is celebrating its 10th anniversary, kicking off the ASI era! 🎉 They're currently on the hunt for AI-related talent, including a principal AI industry reporter, senior video editor, senior editor/editor, and editorial intern. 5.  **Hi3DEval** 📊 is a new, more scientific and comprehensive 3D generation quality assessment system. Launched by the Shanghai AI Lab in collaboration with several universities, it uses a three-tier evaluation protocol (object-level, component-level, and material-theme) to multi-dimensionally analyze the overall form, local structure, and material authenticity of 3D models. Check out the [🔗 Hi3DEval Project Homepage](https://zyh482.github.io/Hi3DEval/) and the [🔗 3D Generation Leaderboard](https://huggingface.co/spaces/3DTopia/3DGen-Leaderboard)!

12. **Fei-Fei Li and Hinton** 🤔 have some differing takes on AI safety. Li's focus is on **design, governance, and values**, while Hinton is more concerned about uncontrolled superintelligence, suggesting we design **AI that cares about humanity**. "Goal misgeneralization" and "instrumental convergence" are highlighted as two core concepts for potential AI risks.
