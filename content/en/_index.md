### **Today's AI Buzz**

1.  **Claude Code:** Just launched its brand-new **"Learning Mode"** 🤖🧠! This cool feature tackles the gnarly problem of **"AI brain atrophy"** – you know, when you rely on AI too much and your skills start to fade. Instead of just spitting out answers, this mode acts like your personal coding "gym coach," nudging you to pause at key steps and get your hands dirty, so you actually master those programming skills. Smart, right?

2.  **Gemma 3 270M:** Google's gone and open-sourced a super-lightweight large model, **Gemma 3 270M** 🐯🚀, with a tiny 270M parameters! Don't let the size fool ya, this little beast crushes **instruction following** and **text structuring** tasks, even outperforming some bigger models. Its low-power vibe means it can run on your phone or a Raspberry Pi, making it perfect for building quick, cheap, and snappy AI apps. How cool is that?

3.  **OpenAI:** CEO Sam Altman is sketching out a massive vision for **OpenAI** 💰🌌. We're talking trillions in investments for data centers and killer new AI hardware, even diving into **brain-computer interfaces**! The goal? To totally shake up search and social media. Altman reckons we're in a pivotal AI moment, kinda like the dot-com bubble days, and the GPT-5 launch is gonna be a "best of times, worst of times" kinda deal. Buckle up!

4.  **Xinzhiyuan:** Happy 10th Anniversary to Xinzhiyuan! 🎉🥳 They're kicking off the ASI (Artificial Superintelligence) era and are on the hunt for AI pros. If you're passionate about AI, they're recruiting for roles like AI Industry Report Chief Writer, Senior Video Editor, Senior Editor/Editor, and Editor Interns. Wanna join the party? 5.  **Hi3DEval:** Get this: the Shanghai AI Lab, hooking up with a bunch of universities, just dropped **Hi3DEval** 😎. It's a more scientific and thorough evaluation system for 3D generation quality. This bad boy uses a three-layer protocol – object-level, component-level, and material theme – to deep-dive into the overall form, local structure, and material realism of 3D models. Pretty neat! Li Feifei is focused on **design, governance, and values**, while Hinton's worried about superintelligence going rogue and suggests we build **AIs that care about humans**. The big concepts here? "Goal misgeneralization" and "instrumental convergence" are the two core ideas behind potential AI risks. Deep thoughts!
