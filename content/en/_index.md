### **Today's AI News**

1.  **Claude Code** just dropped a brand-new **"Learning Mode"**! 🧑‍💻 This nifty feature is designed to combat the dreaded **"AI Brain Atrophy"** that comes from relying too much on AI. Instead of just spitting out answers, it acts like your personal programming "gym coach," guiding you to pause at crucial steps and *actually do* the work yourself. The goal? To truly master coding skills. 💪

2.  **Google's Gemma 3 270M**, an open-source lightweight large model with only 270M parameters, is seriously punching above its weight! 🤯 It's killing it in **instruction following** and **text structuring** tasks, even outperforming some much larger models. Plus, its low-power consumption means it can run on devices like phones or Raspberry Pis, making it perfect for building lean, fast, and budget-friendly AI applications. 🚀

3.  **OpenAI CEO Sam Altman** has painted a massive vision for OpenAI's future: investing trillions of dollars to build data centers and develop cutting-edge AI hardware, even diving into the **brain-computer interface** realm. 🧠 Their goal? To completely shake up search and social media. Altman believes we're in a pivotal AI era, similar to the dot-com bubble, and the upcoming GPT-5 release will mark "the best of times and the worst of times." 💰🌌

4.  **Xinzhiyuan (New Intelligence)** is celebrating its tenth anniversary and kicking off the ASI era! 🎉 They're also on the hunt for top AI talent, with openings for AI industry lead writers, senior video editors, senior editors/editors, and editorial interns. 11. **DeepMind Chief Scientist Denny Zhou** recently broke down the reasoning mechanisms of **Large Language Models (LLMs)** in his Stanford CS25 course. 💡 He explained that LLM reasoning happens by generating a series of **intermediate tokens** and delved into techniques like reinforcement learning fine-tuning. Fascinating stuff! [▶️ Video Demo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ebnX5Ur1hBk)

12. **Fei-Fei Li and Geoffrey Hinton** have some differing views on AI safety. 🤔 Li focuses on **design, governance, and values**, while Hinton is more concerned about superintelligent AI running amok and suggests building **AI that cares about humans**. Key concepts in AI's potential risks are "goal misgeneralization" and "instrumental convergence." It's a critical discussion!
