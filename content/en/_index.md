### **Today's AI News**

1. Claude Code has launched a brand-new "Learning Mode"! 🚀 This mode is designed to tackle the "AI brain drain" problem that comes from relying on AI too much. Instead of just handing out answers, it acts like a personal coding trainer, guiding users to pause at key steps and get hands-on with tasks to truly master their programming skills.

2. Gemma 3 270M, Google's open-source lightweight large model, is a real game-changer! 🤯 With only 270M parameters, it totally crushes tasks like instruction following and text structuring, even outperforming some much bigger models. Its low power consumption means it can run on devices like phones or Raspberry Pis, making it perfect for building nimble, speedy, and budget-friendly AI apps.

3. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman has painted a seriously ambitious vision for OpenAI. 🌌 We're talking trillions of dollars poured into building data centers and developing next-gen AI hardware, even diving into brain-computer interface tech to totally shake up search and social media. Altman believes we're in a pivotal AI era, kind of like the dot-com bubble, and the release of GPT-5 marks "the best of times, and the worst of times." Talk about high stakes! 💰

5. Hi3DEval is a super cool, more scientific, and comprehensive 3D generation quality evaluation system! 😎 It's been rolled out by Shanghai AI Lab in partnership with several universities. This system uses a three-layer evaluation protocol (object-level, component-level, and material theme) to analyze 3D models across multiple dimensions, checking out their overall shape, local structure, and material realism. Pretty neat, right? [🔗 Hi3DEval Project Homepage](https://zyh482.github.io/Hi3DEval/) [🔗 3D Generation Leaderboard](https://huggingface.co/spaces/3DTopia/3DGen-Leaderboard)

Li Feifei and Hinton have some differing takes on AI safety issues. 🤔 Li Feifei is all about the "design, governance, and values," while Hinton is more worried about superintelligence running wild and suggests we design AI that "cares about humanity." The two core concepts for potential AI risks? "Goal misgeneralization" and "instrumental convergence." Fascinating! 🤖
