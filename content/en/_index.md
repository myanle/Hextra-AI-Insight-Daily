### **Today's AI News**

1.  Claude Code has launched a brand new **"Learning Mode"** to tackle the dreaded **"AI brain atrophy"** problem caused by over-reliance on AI. This mode isn't about spoon-feeding answers. Nope! It acts like a coding "personal trainer," guiding users to pause at key steps, get their hands dirty, and truly master programming skills. 🧠💻

2.  Gemma 3 270M, Google's open-source, lightweight large language model, is a total game-changer! Weighing in at just 270M parameters, it crushes **instruction following** and **text structuring** tasks, even outperforming some much larger models. Its low power consumption means it can run on devices like phones or Raspberry Pis, making it perfect for building quick, cost-effective, and nimble AI apps. 🚀📱

3.  OpenAI's CEO, Sam Altman, has laid out an absolutely massive blueprint for the company. He's talking about investing trillions of dollars to build data centers and develop new AI hardware, even diving into the **brain-computer interface** realm with plans to totally shake up search and social media. Altman believes we're smack dab in the middle of a critical AI era, kinda like the dot-com bubble, and the release of GPT-5 signals that "it's the best of times, it's the worst of times." 💰🤯

Pretty cool, huh? 😎 [🔗 Hi3DEval Project Page](https://zyh482.github.io/Hi3DEval/) [🔗 3D Generation Leaderboard](https://huggingface.co/spaces/3DTopia/3DGen-Leaderboard)

11. DeepMind's Chief Scientist, Denny Zhou, gave a mind-blowing lecture at Stanford's CS25 course, explaining the reasoning mechanisms of **Large Language Models (LLMs)**. Pretty neat, right? 🧠 [▶️ Video Demo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ebnX5Ur1hBk)

12. Fei-Fei Li and Hinton have some differing views on AI safety concerns. Li focuses on the critical aspects of **design, governance, and values**, while Hinton is more worried about superintelligence running wild and suggests designing **AI that genuinely cares about humans**. The two big scary concepts for potential AI risks? "Goal misgeneralization" and "instrumental convergence." It's a heavy topic! 🤔
